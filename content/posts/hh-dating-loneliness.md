It is 6:40pm. The reservation is at 7:15. A second shirt is already on the bed because the first one shows underarm marks under the bathroom light.

## 6:40pm - still getting ready

You wipe your palms on a towel, then on your jeans, then on the towel again. The mirror check is whether your face looks shiny and whether the shirt will survive a hug if there is a hug.

Your phone says you still have time. Your hands are already wet before you leave the apartment. That is not a surprise if you have lived with wet palms for years. Anticipatory stress can raise palm sweating before the meetup even starts<sup><a class="cite-ref" href="#src-1">[1]</a></sup>. Under social-evaluative stress, people with primary focal hyperhidrosis also tend to sweat more than people without it<sup><a class="cite-ref" href="#src-2">[2]</a></sup>. On a dating night, that shows up as wet hands while you are still choosing shoes.

You change shirts one more time. You put deodorant on again even though you already did. You stand by the door with your keys and go through the next hour in your head: car, parking, walking in, greeting, sitting close enough that someone might notice damp fabric or a wet handshake.

You check the weather app even though you already know it. Heat makes the underarms worse. Nerves make the palms worse. You pack tissues in a pocket you hope nobody sees you use. You almost put on a darker shirt because stains show less, then talk yourself back into the nicer one because you do not want to look like you dressed for camouflage.

None of that is on the calendar. The calendar only says dinner.

## The cancel text or the early exit

Sometimes you send the text from the hallway. Something about work, or feeling sick, or tomorrow being better. You delete the longer version where you almost tell the truth about your hands and your shirt.

Sometimes you go and make it to the table, then the greeting happens. A handshake, a half-hug, a first touch on the arm. Quality-of-life work on hyperhidrosis has long flagged hand holding, intimate touch, and hand shaking as places people rate their lives as poor before treatment<sup><a class="cite-ref" href="#src-3">[3]</a></sup>. On this night it is simpler than a questionnaire. You feel the wetness meet someone else's skin and you start watching their face for a reaction.

You leave early. You say you have an early morning. You sit in the car and wipe your hands again after a date that ended before the check came.

From lived notes, people talk about being afraid to touch on dates, about excitement making the palms worse, about joking their way past a damp jar lid at dinner because the alternative is naming the real problem. The joke buys a minute. It does not fix the next greeting. Some people finish the meal and still spend the whole time managing whether to put their hands on the table, under the table, or in their lap.

If you canceled instead, you stare at the sent message and wonder whether they believed you. You also feel a short relief that the handshake never happened. That short relief makes it easier to cancel earlier next time.

## The night with nothing on the calendar

You get home earlier than the reservation would have ended. The evening looks empty on paper. There was a plan. Then there was a cancel text or an early exit. Now there is a quiet apartment and a phone you do not want to check.

That is the loneliness nobody schedules. It shows up as another night where dating got dropped because sweat made ordinary contact feel like a test. Across a week it can look like nothing: no dates booked, no follow-up texts, no second try. Friends ask what you are up to. You say you have been busy. You have been busy managing shirts and palms and exits.

The concrete cost is the skipped plans: dinners, walks, and first conversations that never make it past 6:40pm or never make it past the greeting. A month of that and your social life looks like a blank calendar for reasons that never get written down.

If you want one plain next step that is still social and still small, keep the plan and shorten it on purpose. Coffee instead of a long dinner. An outdoor walk instead of a hug-heavy hello. Tell one trusted person ahead of time that your hands get wet when you are nervous so you are not carrying the whole secret alone. None of that dries your hands for good. It is a way to stop letting the cancel text be the default ending.

The loneliness is real even when the calendar stays blank.
